The Prologue

Before There Was a Nation,
There Was a Dream.

For generations, the Muslims of the subcontinent lived under foreign rule and searched for a way to protect their faith, language and future. What began as scattered voices of concern slowly gathered into a shared conviction — that a distinct homeland was not just a political demand, but a necessity for survival and dignity. Poets gave the dream words. Leaders gave it direction. Ordinary people gave it their lives.

Founder of Pakistan

Muhammad Ali Jinnah

"Faith, Unity, Discipline."

Quaid-e-Azam led the constitutional struggle for a separate Muslim homeland with unmatched legal precision and resolve, becoming Pakistan's first Governor-General on 14 August 1947.

☾
Poet-Philosopher

Allama Muhammad Iqbal

"Khudi ko kar buland itna, ke har taqdeer se pehle..."

In his 1930 Allahabad Address, Iqbal gave voice to the idea of a separate Muslim state in North-West India — the intellectual seed from which Pakistan grew.
